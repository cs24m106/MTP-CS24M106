\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:statement}

\subsection{Task Definition}
\paragraph{Motion synthesis objective.}
Given a casual video of a single person, 2D pose sequences are extracted, yielding a conditioning tensor $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times J_{2d} \times 3}$, where $S = 150$ frames (5 s at 30 FPS) and $J_{2d} = 17$ COCO-format joints with per-joint $(x, y, \mathrm{conf})$. Missing or low-confidence joints are masked. The objective is to generate a 3D motion sequence $\mathbf{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times (J_{3d} \cdot C + 4 + 3)}$ comprising:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Joint rotations $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{J_{3d} \times 6}$: $J_{3d} = 24$ joints in 6D rotation space~\cite{6drot2020},
    \item Foot contact labels $\mathbf{f} \in \{0,1\}^4$ for left/right ankle and foot,
    \item Root position $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (global translation).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Control objective.}
The generated motion $\mathbf{M}$, together with curated motion clips from AIST++, serves as reference data for physical imitation. A control policy $\pi_\theta : \mathbf{s}_t \mapsto \mathbf{a}_t$ drives a physically simulated MuJoCo humanoid to reproduce these motions under rigid-body dynamics, formulated as the MDP
\[
    J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T} \gamma^t\, r_t\right],
\]
where state $\mathbf{s}_t$ encodes the character's recent pose history, action $\mathbf{a}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{28}$ specifies PD controller targets, and reward $r_t$ is derived from adversarial discriminator scores rather than manually engineered functions.

\subsection{Motion Corpus and Data Preparation}
\label{sec:vimo_data}

\textbf{AIST++ Dataset.} The AIST++ benchmark~\cite{aist2021} provides 1,408 sequences of 3D dance motions across 10 genres with synchronized multi-view video and annotations. Each sequence stores SMPL pose parameters ($\text{smpl\_poses} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 24 \times 3}$ axis-angle) and global translation ($\text{smpl\_trans} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$). Nine calibrated camera views supply 2D keypoints at 1920$\times$1080 px, 60 FPS, in COCO-17 format with per-joint confidence.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{Pictures/keypoints.png}
    \caption{COCO 17-keypoint skeleton order used for 2D pose conditioning.}
    \label{fig:keypoints}
\end{figure}

\textbf{2D Pose Extraction.} Pose extractors such as OpenPose and AlphaPose provide per-joint confidence scores used to mask unreliable detections; in our pipeline, the confidence field is used directly for reliability-aware conditioning~\cite{alphapose2022}. In multi-view scenarios the highest-confidence detection per joint is selected, and optional temporal smoothing is applied conservatively to preserve micro-motions~\cite{vimo2024}.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{Pictures/posedata.png}
    \caption{Representative pose estimator output with confidence-masked joints.}
    \label{fig:posedata}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Preprocessing.} Raw sequences are resampled from 60 FPS to 30 FPS (every second frame). Each sequence is partitioned into non-overlapping 150-frame clips. The conditioning tensor $\mathbf{p}$ is normalized by centering relative to the root joint, removing absolute pixel coordinates and enabling generalisation across cameras and video scales. Ground-truth 3D motion is encoded as $150 \times 151$ per clip: 144 dims for 6D rotations~\cite{6drot2020}, 4 dims for foot contact labels (derived from near-zero vertical foot/toe velocities), and 3 dims for root translation. Sequences in an ignore list and SMPL axis-angle rotations are orthogonalized to 6D form~\cite{6drot2020}.

\subsection{Simulation Character and Reference Motion Format}
\label{sec:simchar}

\textbf{MuJoCo Humanoid.} The simulation character is a rigid-body humanoid in MuJoCo~\cite{mujoco2012} with 15 bodies and 28 actuated DOF. Table~\ref{tab:skeleton} compares it to the SMPL kinematic model used by the motion generator.

\begin{table}[htb]
\centering
\caption{SMPL kinematic model vs.\ MuJoCo simulation humanoid.}
\label{tab:skeleton}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Property & \textbf{SMPL} & \textbf{MuJoCo Humanoid} \\
\midrule
Total joints/bodies & 24 & 15 \\
Actuated DOF & 72 (axis-angle) & 28 (hinge/ball) \\
Root representation & translation + axis-angle & 3D position + quaternion \\
Coordinate system & Y-up (right-handed) & Z-up (right-handed) \\
Collarbones & explicit (L/R\_Collar) & fused into torso \\
Hands & articulated wrists & rigid end-effectors \\
Spine & 3 segments (Spine1--3) & single torso body \\
Contact model & none (mesh) & rigid-body + friction \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

    \centering

The DOF reduction from 72 to 28 collapses the three spine segments, collarbones, and detailed hand joints into simplified rigid bodies while preserving the major bilateral chains (arms, legs). This simplification is necessary for stable physics simulation: attempting to control 72 independent motors on a ragdoll-style body leads to instability~\cite{deepmimic2018}.

\textbf{Reference Motion Format.} The Composite Motion framework expects reference data in DeepMimic JSON format---a per-frame array of the character's full \texttt{qpos} vector. Each frame records:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pelvis world position $\mathbf{p}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and orientation $\mathbf{q}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion, $[x,y,z,w]$),
    \item Local quaternion orientations for the 14 non-root bodies.
\end{itemize}
This format directly encodes the MuJoCo generalized coordinates, ensuring compatibility with the pretrained discriminator checkpoints and reference-motion samplers.

\textbf{SMPL-to-Humanoid Data Bridge.} ViMo-Flow outputs and AIST++ mocap clips are both stored in SMPL representation. A retargeting converter maps these to the DeepMimic format through coordinate transformation, height scaling, bind-rotation correction, and chain composition. Key considerations motivating the conversion are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item SMPL joint axes and coordinate conventions are incompatible with MuJoCo's Z-up frame.
    \item Bone length proportions differ between the SMPL neutral body and the MuJoCo humanoid XML.
    \item Multi-joint SMPL chains (e.g., three spine segments) must be composed into single MuJoCo bodies with appropriate bind corrections.
\end{itemize}
